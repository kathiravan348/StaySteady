Lifecycle Step Who Does What? Where You See It Normal User Benefit
1. Discovery Data Providers feed stock quotes, foreign exchange rates, and financial news into the app. Watchlists / Workspace / Feed Everything is consolidated; you don't need 5 browser tabs open.
2. Strategy Testing You define rules (e.g. "Buy ETF if down 5% from 50-day average") and run a backtest. Research > Backtest Know whether an idea actually made money historically BEFORE risking real capital.
3. Signal Detection The automated engine evaluates live quotes against your active strategy rules. Trading > Signals No need to stare at charts all day; the platform alerts you when conditions trigger.
4. Safety Check The platform checks daily loss limits, maximum position caps, cash reserves, and employer blackout dates. Risk > Limits & Compliance Guarantees that a buggy formula or rogue bot cannot wipe out your account.
5. Human Review The order waits in the queue with estimated cost and portfolio impact. You click Approve or Reject. Trading > Approval Queue You retain 100% control over every dollar leaving your broker account.
6. Execution & Tracking Order is filled at your broker. The lot is added to your tax ledger, and value updates automatically. Portfolio > Holdings Shows exact profit, dividend yield, and tax liabilities in your chosen home currency.

🎓 Stock Market & Asset Classes Explained

Plain English Primer
📈 1. What is a Stock (Equity / Share)?
  • Ownership Slice: When you buy 1 share of a company (like Apple, Microsoft, or Tata Motors), you become a legal part-owner of that business.
  • Way 1 to Make Money (Price Growth): If the company invents great products and profits grow, other investors will pay more for your shares (Capital Appreciation).
  • Way 2 to Make Money (Dividends): Many profitable companies distribute excess profits directly as cash into your brokerage account every 3 months.
  • Risk Level: Moderate to High — Individual companies can stumble, lose market share, or even go bankrupt.
🧺 2. What is a Mutual Fund?
  • Pooling Money: Thousands of regular people pool their small monthly savings together into one giant pot managed by a professional Fund House (AMC).
  • Professional Manager: A licensed fund manager and research team pick 50 to 100 different companies for the fund so you don't have to pick individual stocks.
  • Net Asset Value (NAV): Mutual funds do NOT trade in real-time. All buy/sell orders are processed once a day after market closes at that day's official NAV price.
  • Active vs. Passive: Active funds hire managers to beat the market (higher fees: 1-2%); Passive Index funds simply copy market indexes (ultra-low fees: 0.1%).
⚡ 3. What is an ETF (Exchange Traded Fund)?
  • A Basket That Trades Like a Single Stock: An ETF holds hundreds of top companies (e.g. all 500 companies in the S&P 500, or top 50 in Nifty 50).
  • Real-Time Live Trading: Unlike a mutual fund, you can buy and sell an ETF instantly in 1 second during live market hours just like a regular stock.
  • Rock-Bottom Fees: ETFs often have fees as low as 0.03% to 0.15% per year, meaning more of your profit stays in your pocket.
  • Instant Diversification: Buying 1 share of an S&P 500 ETF spreads your risk across 500 top global corporations instantly.
🪙 4. What is Gold & Commodities?
  • The Crisis Shield: Gold produces no dividends or products, but has preserved purchasing power for 5,000 years. When wars or hyperinflation hit, gold shines.
  • Physical Gold (Jewelry & Coins): Incurs high making charges (10-20%), safety locker fees, and risk of theft.
  • Sovereign Gold Bonds (SGB): Government-backed gold bonds that pay you 2.5% annual interest on top of gold price appreciation, with zero storage risk.
  • Gold ETFs / Digital Gold: Buy and sell 1 gram of gold on your phone with live market pricing and zero making charges.
🏛️ 5. What is a Stock Exchange (NYSE, NASDAQ, LSE, NSE)?
  • The Central Digital Marketplace: The high-speed electronic exchange matching millions of buyers and sellers worldwide.
  • The Order Book: Displays the highest price buyers are willing to pay (Bids) vs. the lowest price sellers are willing to accept (Asks).
  • The Spread: The difference between Bid and Ask. High-volume stocks have tiny spreads ($0.01); rare stocks have wide spreads.
  • T+1 Settlement: When you execute a trade today (Day T), official legal transfer of shares to your depository and money to the seller finishes by the next day (T+1).
🛡️ 6. Bonds, Fixed Deposits & Cash
  • Bonds (Lending Money): You lend money to the Government (Treasury Bonds) or safe corporations. They pay you guaranteed fixed interest every 6 months.
  • Liquid Funds / Bank FDs: Safe emergency money parked with zero volatility. Gives you peace of mind and dry powder to buy market dips.
  • Capital Protection: While stocks fluctuate daily, government bonds provide steady, predictable income.

⏱️ Trading Styles Explained (Intraday vs. Swing vs. Long-Term)

Know Your Style Before Risking Money
⚡ Style A: Intraday Trading (Day Trading)
  • Holding Period: Minutes to Hours. All trades are closed before market bells ring (3:30 PM / 4:00 PM). Zero overnight risk.
  • Leverage & Margin: Brokers let you trade with 5x leverage (borrowing $4,000 to trade with $1,000). A 2% gain gives you 10% profit; a 2% loss wipes out 10% of your capital!
  • Time Commitment: Full-time screen staring. Requires lightning-fast emotional control, level-2 order book reading, and charting tools.
  • ⚠️ The Sobering Reality: Official government regulatory studies show that over 93% of retail intraday traders lose money after paying broker commissions and exchange taxes.
🌊 Style B: Swing & Momentum Trading
  • Holding Period: 2 Days to 4 Weeks. Catches short-to-medium price waves and earnings momentum bounces.
  • How it Works: Buys stocks breaking out above key resistance (e.g. 50-day moving averages) and sells when momentum slows down.
  • Overnight Exposure: Exposed to news gaps (if negative news breaks overnight, stock can open 5% lower the next morning).
  • Where StaySteady Helps: StaySteady's automated Signals Feed and Risk Limits are engineered specifically to automate swing logic safely without emotional panic.
🌳 Style C: Long-Term Investing (Buy & Hold / Compounding)
  • Holding Period: 3 Years to 10+ Years. Buying high-quality companies or broad market index ETFs and letting them compound peacefully.
  • The Magic of Compounding: Einstein called compound interest the 8th wonder of the world. Your profits earn profits.
    • Investing $500 per month at a 12% average annual return becomes ~$115,000 in 10 years!
    • In 20 years, that same $500/month turns into ~$495,000! (Your actual deposited cash was only $120,000; the rest is pure compounded profit!).
  • Tax Advantages: Qualifying for Long-Term Capital Gains (LTCG) tax rates which are significantly lower than short-term trading taxes.
  • Stress Level: Zero Daily Stress — You do not care if the stock market drops 1% on Tuesday; you know that quality businesses grow over decades.

FORMULA 01 Costing Average / Dollar-Cost Averaging (DCA)
Core Long-Term Wealth Formula
Average Cost per Share = Total Money Spent ÷ Total Shares Bought DCA Formula
💡 Plain English Explanation
  • What it is: Instead of trying to guess the "lowest market bottom", you invest a fixed sum of money (e.g. $500) on the exact same date every month.
  • Why it works: When the stock market crashes, your $500 automatically buys more shares. When the market is expensive, your $500 buys fewer shares. Your average cost per share automatically drops!
🔢 Step-by-Step Worked Numerical Example:
  • • Month 1 (Market High): Stock Price = $100 → You spend $600 → You get 6.0 shares ($600 ÷ 100).
  • • Month 2 (Market Crash -50%): Stock Price drops to $50 → You spend $600 → You get 12.0 shares ($600 ÷ 50).
  • • Month 3 (Partial Recovery): Stock Price rebounds to $75 → You spend $600 → You get 8.0 shares ($600 ÷ 75).
  • • Total Cash Invested: $600 + $600 + $600 = $1,800.
  • • Total Shares Owned: 6 + 12 + 8 = 26 shares.
  • • Your Average Cost per Share: $1,800 ÷ 26 = $69.23!
  • • Current Portfolio Value at $75: 26 shares × $75 = $1,950 (+$150 Profit), even though the stock is still 25% LOWER than where you started ($100)!
🛠️ How to Build This Strategy in StaySteady:

In Strategy Editor (/research/editor): Scope = Broad Market ETFs (e.g. VOO or SPY) → Trigger = Calendar Schedule (1st of every month at 10:00 AM) → Order = Market Buy → Sizing = Fixed Dollar Amount ($600). Let it run on autopilot!

FORMULA 02 Simple Moving Average (SMA) & Golden Cross
Trend Following Model
SMA_n = (P_1 + P_2 + P_3 + ... + P_n) ÷ n n = Number of Days
💡 Plain English Explanation
  • What it is: The arithmetic average of closing prices over the last n days. It filters out daily chaotic price noise to show the true direction of the tide.
  • The 200-day SMA: The benchmark line followed by institutional funds. If price is above the 200 SMA, the asset is in a long-term bull market.
  • The Golden Cross: When the faster 50-day SMA crosses above the slower 200-day SMA, indicating major bullish buying momentum.
🔢 Step-by-Step Worked Numerical Example (5-Day SMA):
  • • Daily closing prices over the past 5 trading days: Day 1: $102, Day 2: $105, Day 3: $103, Day 4: $108, Day 5: $112.
  • • Sum of past 5 days = 102 + 105 + 103 + 108 + 112 = $530.00.
  • • 5-day SMA = $530 ÷ 5 = $106.00.
  • • Since today's price ($112) is well above the 5-day SMA ($106), short-term momentum is strongly upward.
🛠️ How to Build This Strategy in StaySteady:

In Strategy Editor (/research/editor): Entry Condition = SMA(50) Crosses Above SMA(200) → Exit Condition = SMA(50) Crosses Below SMA(200) OR Trailing Stop -5%. This captures long-term multi-month bull trends while keeping you out of bear market crashes!

FORMULA 03 Exponential Moving Average (EMA)
Fast-Response Momentum
Multiplier k = 2 ÷ (n + 1) | EMA_today = (Price × k) + (EMA_yesterday × (1 - k)) Weighted Average
💡 Plain English Explanation
  • Why use EMA over SMA? In a simple SMA, a price move from 50 days ago has the exact same weight as today's price. The EMA gives the greatest mathematical weight to the most recent trading days, reacting faster to sharp breakouts.
  • Popular Settings: 9 EMA and 21 EMA are widely used for swing trading breakouts.
🔢 Step-by-Step Worked Numerical Example (9-Day EMA):
  • • Step 1: Calculate Multiplier k = 2 ÷ (9 + 1) = 0.20 (meaning 20% weight to today, 80% to history).
  • • Step 2: Yesterday's 9-day EMA was $100.00. Today the stock jumps to $110.00.
  • • Step 3: $\text{EMA}_{\text{today}} = (110 \times 0.20) + (100 \times 0.80) = 22.00 + 80.00 = \mathbf{\$102.00}$.
  • • Notice: The EMA quickly adapted to $102.00, whereas a 9-day SMA would have only nudged up to ~$101.10.
FORMULA 04 Relative Strength Index (RSI) Formula
Overbought / Oversold Oscillator
RS = Avg Gain ÷ Avg Loss | RSI = 100 - [ 100 ÷ (1 + RS) ] Scale: 0 to 100
💡 Plain English Explanation
  • The Rubber Band Effect: Measures the speed and change of price moves over 14 days.
  • RSI > 70 (Overbought): The stock has rallied too far, too fast. Buyers are exhausted; high probability of a short-term pullback.
  • RSI < 30 (Oversold): Extreme panic selling. Sellers have exhausted their shares; high probability of a relief bounce.
🔢 Step-by-Step Worked Numerical Example:
  • • Step 1: Over 14 trading days, calculate all green days and red days.
  • • Average Gain on up days = +$1.80. Average Loss on down days = -$0.60.
  • • Step 2: Relative Strength $RS = 1.80 ÷ 0.60 = \mathbf{3.0}$.
  • • Step 3: $\text{RSI} = 100 - [ 100 ÷ (1 + 3.0) ] = 100 - [ 100 ÷ 4.0 ] = 100 - 25 = \mathbf{75.0}$.
  • • Conclusion: RSI is 75 (Overbought). Do NOT buy here; wait for a price cooling period!
🛠️ The "Oversold Rebound" Strategy in StaySteady:

In Strategy Editor (/research/editor): Entry Condition = RSI(14) < 30 AND Close > SMA(200). (Only buy oversold dips if the overall stock is in a solid multi-year bull trend!). Exit Condition = RSI(14) > 65 OR Profit > +10%.

FORMULA 05 Moving Average Convergence Divergence (MACD)
Trend-Momentum Combination
MACD Line = EMA_12 - EMA_26 | Signal Line = EMA_9(MACD Line) | Histogram = MACD - Signal Momentum Histogram
💡 Plain English Explanation
  • What it does: Compares short-term momentum (12 EMA) with long-term momentum (26 EMA).
  • Bullish Crossover: When the MACD line crosses above the Signal line (Histogram turns green / positive), buyers have taken control.
  • Bearish Crossover: When the MACD line drops below the Signal line (Histogram turns red / negative), sellers have taken control.
🔢 Step-by-Step Worked Numerical Example:
  • • Step 1: Calculate 12-day EMA = $152.00. Calculate 26-day EMA = $148.00.
  • • Step 2: $\text{MACD Line} = 152.00 - 148.00 = \mathbf{+\$4.00}$.
  • • Step 3: The 9-day Signal EMA of MACD is currently +$2.50.
  • • Step 4: $\text{Histogram} = 4.00 - 2.50 = \mathbf{+\$1.50}$.
  • • Result: A positive $1.50 histogram confirms strong accelerating upward trend momentum.
FORMULA 06 Bollinger Bands & Volatility Squeeze
Volatility Envelope
Middle = SMA_20 | Upper = SMA_20 + (2 × σ) | Lower = SMA_20 - (2 × σ) σ = Standard Deviation
💡 Plain English Explanation
  • Statistical Price Envelope: Based on normal distribution statistics, stock prices stay between the Upper and Lower bands approximately 95% of the time.
  • The Bollinger Squeeze: When the upper and lower bands narrow to an ultra-tight channel, market volatility has dried up. A massive explosive breakout is imminent!
🔢 Step-by-Step Worked Numerical Example:
  • • 20-day SMA (Middle Band) = $100.00.
  • • Standard Deviation ($\sigma$) of the past 20 daily closes = $3.50.
  • • Upper Band = $100 + (2 \times 3.50) = 100 + 7.00 = \mathbf{\$107.00}$.
  • • Lower Band = $100 - (2 \times 3.50) = 100 - 7.00 = \mathbf{\$93.00}$.
  • • Trading Rule: If price touches $93.00 and forms a green bullish candle, it indicates a statistical oversold bounce opportunity back toward the mean ($100).
FORMULA 07 Average True Range (ATR) & Volatility Stop-Loss
Intelligent Noise-Free Exits
TR = max[ (H - L), |H - C_prev|, |L - C_prev| ] | Stop-Loss = Entry Price - (2 × ATR_14) Volatility Exit
💡 Plain English Explanation
  • Why amateur stop-losses fail: Normal people arbitrarily place a stop-loss at "1% below entry". A volatile stock easily drops 1.5% in 10 minutes from random market noise, triggering your stop before soaring 10% higher!
  • The ATR Solution: ATR measures how many dollars the stock moves on a typical day. Setting your stop-loss at 2 × ATR guarantees that ordinary daily noise will not kick you out of a winning trade!
🔢 Step-by-Step Worked Numerical Example:
  • • You buy Nvidia (NVDA) at $120.00.
  • • NVDA's 14-day ATR is $4.20 (meaning on an average day, it moves $4.20 up or down).
  • • Intelligent 2-ATR Stop-Loss = $\$120.00 - (2 \times \$4.20) = \$120.00 - \$8.40 = \mathbf{\$111.60}$.
  • • Any move above $111.60 is classified as normal market turbulence. Only if price closes below $111.60 has the structural thesis genuinely broken!
FORMULA 08 Position Sizing: The 1% Golden Risk Rule
Crucial Account Survival Math
Shares to Buy = (Total Portfolio Capital × Risk %) ÷ (Entry Price - Stop-Loss Price) Zero Bankruptcy Risk
💡 Plain English Explanation
  • The Secret of Institutional Money Managers: You do NOT decide how many shares to buy based on excitement. You decide based on how much money you are willing to lose if wrong.
  • The 1% Rule: On any single trade, your maximum loss if your stop-loss hits must NEVER exceed 1% of your total net account value.
🔢 Step-by-Step Worked Numerical Example:
  • • Your Total Portfolio: $50,000.
  • • Max Allowable Loss (1%): $50,000 × 0.01 = $500.00 max risk.
  • • Stock Setup: Entry Price = $100.00 | Technical Stop-Loss = $95.00.
  • • Risk Per Share: $100.00 - $95.00 = $5.00 per share.
  • • Exact Number of Shares to Buy: $500.00 ÷ $5.00 = 100 shares ($10,000 capital allocated).
  • • The Proof: If the trade completely fails and crashes to $95.00, your loss is 100 shares × $5.00 = $500.00. Your remaining portfolio is $49,500 (99% intact!). You can make 10 consecutive mistakes and still be totally fine!
FORMULA 09 Risk-to-Reward Ratio (R:R)
Probability & Profitability Math
R:R = (Target Price - Entry Price) ÷ (Entry Price - Stop-Loss Price) Target 1:2 or 1:3
💡 Plain English Explanation
  • Why amateurs go broke with an 80% win rate: They make $50 on 8 winning trades (+$400), but on their 2 losing trades, they refuse to sell and lose $600 (-$1,200). Net result: broke despite winning 8 out of 10 times!
  • The Pro Equation: Professional quantitative investors only take trades with at least a 1:2 or 1:3 Risk-to-Reward ratio.
🔢 How to be Profitable with only a 35% Win Rate (1:3 R:R):
  • • Risk per trade = $100. Target profit per trade = $300 (1:3 R:R).
  • • You make 10 trades and you are WRONG 65% of the time (7 losses, only 3 wins):
  • • 3 Winning Trades × $300 = +$900 Gross Profit.
  • • 7 Losing Trades × $100 = -$700 Gross Loss.
  • • Net Profit = +$200.00! You made money while being wrong almost twice as often as you were right!
FORMULA 10 Compound Annual Growth Rate (CAGR)
Multi-Year Growth Measurement
CAGR = [ (Ending Value ÷ Starting Value) ^ (1 ÷ n) ] - 1 n = Years
🔢 Step-by-Step Worked Numerical Example:
  • • You invested $10,000 in 2018. In 2025 (7 years later), your portfolio has grown to $24,883.
  • • Step 1: Ending ÷ Starting = $24,883 ÷ $10,000 = 2.4883.
  • • Step 2: Exponent = 1 ÷ 7 years = 0.142857.
  • • Step 3: $(2.4883)^{0.142857} = 1.139$.
  • • Step 4: $1.139 - 1 = \mathbf{0.139} = \mathbf{13.9\%\text{ CAGR}}$.
FORMULA 11 Maximum Drawdown (MDD)
Downside Pain Metric
MDD = [ (Trough Value - Peak Value) ÷ Peak Value ] × 100 Deepest Dip (%)
🔢 Step-by-Step Worked Numerical Example:
  • • In July, your account reached an all-time high (Peak) of $100,000.
  • • During a market correction in September, it dropped to a low point (Trough) of $78,000 before recovering to new highs in December.
  • • $\text{MDD} = [ (78000 - 100000) ÷ 100000 ] \times 100 = [ -22000 ÷ 100000 ] \times 100 = \mathbf{-22.0\%}$.
  • • Rule of Thumb: If a strategy's historical backtest shows an MDD of -35%, ask yourself if you have the psychological stomach to see your $100k account drop to $65k without panicking!
FORMULA 12 Sharpe Ratio (Risk-Adjusted Return)
Return Quality Score
Sharpe Ratio = (Portfolio Return - Risk-Free Rate) ÷ Portfolio Volatility (σ) Benchmark > 1.0
🔢 Comparing Two Strategies:
  • • Risk-Free Government Rate = 4.0%.
  • • Strategy A: Made 18% return, but with extreme roller-coaster swings ($\sigma = 20\%$). Sharpe = $(18 - 4) ÷ 20 = \mathbf{0.70}$ (Poor risk-adjusted return).
  • • Strategy B: Made 14% return, but with calm, smooth growth ($\sigma = 6\%$). Sharpe = $(14 - 4) ÷ 6 = \mathbf{1.67}$ (Exceptional institutional grade!).
  • • Winner: Strategy B is far superior because it generates steady wealth with low anxiety.
FORMULA 13 Profit Factor
Gross Efficiency Score
Profit Factor = Total Gross Profits from Winners ÷ Total Gross Losses from Losers Target > 1.5

  • • Meaning: For every $1 your strategy lost, it successfully brought back $2.00 in profit.

🔤 Master Abbreviations & Financial Indicators Table

Complete A-Z Quick Reference
Term & Acronym Category What It Stands For Plain English Explanation Real-World Example & Rule of Thumb
P/E Fundamental Price-to-Earnings Ratio How many dollars you pay for every $1 of annual profit the company makes. If a stock is $100 and earns $5/year profit, P/E = 20. Rule: Under 15 is historically cheap; over 35 is expensive (growth expectation).
P/B Fundamental Price-to-Book Ratio Comparing stock price to physical liquidation assets (cash, property, factories). P/B < 1 means you can buy the company for less than the cost of its physical assets if liquidated tomorrow.
EPS Fundamental Earnings Per Share Total company net profit divided by the total number of shares in existence. If net profit is $10M and there are 2M shares, EPS = $5.00. Rule: Growing EPS year-over-year indicates a thriving business.
ROE Fundamental Return on Equity How efficiently management generates profit from shareholder capital invested. ROE of 20% means management generated $20 profit for every $100 of equity. Rule: Look for ROE > 15%.
Div Yield Fundamental Dividend Yield (%) The annual cash percentage deposited into your account just for holding the stock. A $100 stock that pays $4 in dividends each year has a 4% dividend yield. Ideal for steady passive income.
Market Cap Fundamental Market Capitalization Total market price tag to buy 100% of all company shares. Large Cap: > $10B (Safe, stable giants like Microsoft).
Mid Cap: $2B - $10B (Fast growing).
Small Cap: < $2B (High growth, high risk).
RSI Technical Relative Strength Index Momentum oscillator (0 to 100) measuring how aggressively buyers or sellers pushed price. RSI > 70 (Overbought): Price rallied too fast; pullback ahead.
RSI < 30 (Oversold): Panic selling extreme; potential bargain bounce.
MACD Technical Moving Average Convergence Divergence Trend-following momentum indicator showing the relationship between two moving averages. When the MACD line crosses above the Signal line, it signals fresh bullish buying momentum.
SMA Technical Simple Moving Average The smoothed average closing price over a set period (e.g. 50-day or 200-day average). 200-day SMA: The ultimate long-term health line. Price above 200 SMA indicates healthy bull trend.
EMA Technical Exponential Moving Average Weighted moving average that gives higher priority to the most recent days. Short-term swing traders use 9 EMA and 21 EMA to catch rapid trend reversals.
ATR Technical Average True Range Measures typical daily volatility in dollar terms. Set stop-losses at 2 × ATR to prevent getting stopped out by random daily market noise.
Beta (β) Risk Metric Market Volatility Sensitivity How violently the stock swings compared to broader market index (S&P 500 = 1.0). Beta = 1.5: Swings 50% wider than market; Beta = 0.6: Defensive, low volatility asset.
Alpha (α) Risk Metric Excess Return Beat Extra profit generated by your strategy beyond simple market index growth. If market makes 10% and strategy makes 14%, Alpha is +4.0%.
CAGR Risk Metric Compound Annual Growth Rate Smoothed annual return rate as if investment grew steadily year-over-year. Normalizes wild swings (+20%, -10%, +15%) into a single annualized return figure.
NAV Fund Metric Net Asset Value The official price of 1 unit of a Mutual Fund, calculated daily at market close. Total Fund Assets minus Liabilities divided by total units issued to investors.
SIP Fund Metric Systematic Investment Plan Automating a fixed dollar investment (e.g. $200) every month on the same date. Takes away market timing stress via automatic Dollar Cost Averaging.
SL & TP Order Rules Stop-Loss & Target Price Pre-set safety exit orders entered into the system to cut a loss or lock in profits. Bought at $100: Stop-Loss at $95 (Max 5% loss) and Target Price at $115 (+15% profit target).
FIFO / LIFO Tax & Lots First-In-First-Out / Last-In-First-Out Accounting methods determining which purchase receipt is sold first when closing lots. FIFO sells your oldest shares first to qualify for lower Long-Term Capital Gains tax discounts.
VaR Risk Metric Value at Risk Statistical forecast of maximum expected loss on 95% or 99% of normal trading days. "95% 1-Day VaR = $2,500" means on 19 out of 20 days, loss will not exceed $2,500.
Spread Exchange Bid-Ask Difference The gap between what buyers offer and what sellers demand on the exchange order book. Liquid stocks have $0.01 spreads. Illiquid penny stocks have wide spreads that act like hidden taxes.
